BP, Gradient Descent, and ADMM

The Tanner graph and ADMM state come from the King’s Land three-symbol example: c = (0.1, 0.6, 0.9), check A joins symbols 1–2, and check B joins symbols 2–3. For this new comparison, ci is explicitly set equal to P(bi = 1 | yi) to form the BP input LLR and the bipolar GD initialization. The serial A-then-B reveal does not change the frozen snapshot used by any calculation.

What the synchronized beats do—and do not—claim

The panels share a graph, mapped initial evidence, and a presentation clock. They do not implement one objective: BP performs probabilistic inference from LLRs; GD reduces the objective function Jp; ADMM minimizes ½‖b − c‖² under parity-polytope constraints. Only BP’s Q and U are messages. Each GD edge quantity is one term in the partial derivative of Jp with respect to a symbol value, while ADMM’s replica variables and scaled dual variables persist between rounds. Synchronized placement is therefore not a claim of equal arithmetic, state, operation count, or complexity.

Space plays or pauses; the arrow keys step. One iteration ends with the hard word 011 in all three panels. That word fails check A. In ADMM, legal projected replicas after one round do not mean that the shared hard word passes or that the continuous variables have converged.
