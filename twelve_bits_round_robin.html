BP, Gradient Descent, and ADMM on Twelve Bits

The six checks sit at the corners of a hexagon, and each bit sits on the line between its two checks, so every bit has two checks and every check has four bits. The graph has cycles: BP’s two messages out of a bit differ from iteration 2 on, GD adds two derivative terms for each bit, and ADMM keeps two replicas of each bit. The sent word 011000111010 arrives with bits 0 and 7 wrong. Each press of Next runs one turn of one method, in the order BP → GD → ADMM; a method stops at the first word that passes all six checks, or after three iterations.

The code, the noise, the symbols, and what the turns do not claim

Check ck joins bits k − 1, k, 6 + (k − 2) and 6 + k (indices of the first two mod 6, of the last two mod 6 within 6–11): bits 0–5 lie on the hexagon’s sides and bits 6–11 on the two triangles inside it. These are the edges of an octahedron, so the codewords are the even subgraphs of the octahedron, the smallest being a triangle (minimum distance 3). The six checks are not independent: any five of them imply the sixth.

The sent word was mapped to ±1 (bit 1 → +1), Gaussian noise with σ = 0.9 was added (Python’s random.Random(1).gauss), y was rounded to two decimals, and L = 2y/σ² was rounded to two decimals. The starting worksheets list both.

BP owns L, Q, t, U, B; GD owns x, x̃, r, g, η; ADMM owns p, b, z, u, ρ, β, Sc. All three share the checks, the matrix H and the hard word b̂.

The methods share the graph, the evidence and the turn order, not one objective. BP computes LLR messages; GD lowers Jp = Σc(1 − Πj∈N(c)xj)² with η = 0.25 (a satisfied four-bit check has product +1); ADMM minimizes ½‖b − p‖² with each check’s bits kept in the parity polytope PP4, with ρ = 1, as on the six-bit and three-symbol pages. Only BP’s Q and U are messages. Taking equal turns is not a claim of equal arithmetic, state or cost.
